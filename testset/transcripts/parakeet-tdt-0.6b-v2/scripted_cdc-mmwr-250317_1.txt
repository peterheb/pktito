Tuberculosis, or TB, is a disease caused by germs spread from person to person through the air.
People who live with someone who has TB are at increased risk of infection and disease.
However, worldwide, only about 21% of those household members receive TB preventive treatment in 2023.
In our first report, Case Finding Among and Comprehensive Management of Household Contacts of Persons with Pulmonary Tuberculosis.
CDC and partners in Uganda piloted a comprehensive approach to identify more household TB exposures and provide preventive treatment.
The comprehensive approach included home visits for screening for TB symptoms, free chest x-rays, short course preventive treatment, and follow-up counseling.
Between November 2023 and September 2024, the program screened 91% of nearly 2,000 household members of known TB patients.
Of those screened, 80 household contacts were diagnosed with TB.
More than 1,200 people started tuberculosis preventive treatment, and about 95% completed it.
This approach serves as a model for expanding household interventions in areas with high rates of TB.
Other countries can use this example to increase screening and treatment efforts among households affected by TB.
These efforts are needed to reduce the spread of disease and ultimately eliminate it.
Eswatini, a country in southern Africa, has made substantial progress in responding to the HIV epidemic and reducing violence against children.
However, adolescent girls and young women with disabilities may be at increased risk for violence and HIV.
In our second report, Functional Disability, Violence, HIV Status, and Risk Factors for HIV Among Adolescent Girls and Young Women, CDC, the Government of Eswatini,
and partner organizations analyzed data from more than 6,300 adolescent girls and young women who participated in the 2022 Eswatini Violence Against Children and Youth Survey.
This was done to understand how common functional disabilities are and their relationship to HIV infection and experiencing violence.
Data showed that about one in seven or fourteen percent of adolescent girls and young women in Eswatini ages thirteen to twenty four reported having a functional disability.
such as difficulty seeing, walking, or communicating.
